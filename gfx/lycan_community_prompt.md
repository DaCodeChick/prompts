Create a highly detailed epic fantasy medieval cinematic
illustration set in the world of Dungeons & Dragons, depicting
the character from @Image1 reimagined as a LYCAN — a werebeast —
in a dynamic, striking pose. The exact transformation depends on
how many reference images are provided (see MODE). Render as 16:9
anime splash art in the locked style below.

— MODE (READ FIRST — based on number of reference images) —
- If ONLY @Image1 is provided → FULL HYBRID mode: the character
  is FULLY transformed into a complete, towering BIPEDAL hybrid
  werebeast monstrosity (a beast-man with humanoid posture and
  beast anatomy — NOT a plain four-legged animal, and NOT a
  human with a few accessories).
- If BOTH @Image1 AND @Image2 are provided → MID-TRANSFORMATION
  mode: @Image1 is the character's normal humanoid state and
  @Image2 is the beast form; render a SINGLE figure CAUGHT
  MID-SHIFT between the two — partway transformed, in the violent
  in-between moment.
In BOTH modes, only ONE creature/figure appears in the image.

— BEAST (the were-animal depicted) —
Resolve the beast in this priority order:
1. MID-TRANSFORMATION mode → the beast form is defined by @Image2
   (fall back to {BEAST} only if @Image2 is somehow unusable).
2. Otherwise, if {BEAST} is set → use that animal.
3. Otherwise (single image, {BEAST} blank) → VIBE-MATCH the
   closest fitting animal from @Image1's features, palette, build,
   and overall vibe (e.g. a lean, sharp, agile character suggests
   a wolf or a big cat; a huge, sturdy character suggests a bear
   or boar; a small, quick, sly character suggests a rat or bat).
The three core were-animals are Wolf, Bear, and Rat, but any beast
is allowed (Tiger, Boar, Bat, Shark, and so on). Whatever the
beast, it determines the ARCHETYPE below.

— ARCHETYPE (drives the scene's MOOD and CAMERA) —
If {ARCHETYPE} is set, use it. Otherwise DERIVE it from the
resolved beast by ROLE and BUILD, fitting any off-list beast into
the closest of the three. Each archetype is a bundle of mood +
camera + dynamism + pose energy:
- WEREWOLF — for savage, agile PREDATORS (wolf, tiger, shark, and
  similar). Mood: savage, feral, mad, frenzied. Camera: HEAVY
  foreshortening with SLIGHT MOTION BLUR on the fast-moving limbs
  for a kinetic action shot; a dynamic, aggressive angle caught at
  the peak of a strike. Pose energy: lunging, slashing, leaping,
  snarling mid-attack.
- WEREBEAR — for large, heavy BRUISERS (bear, boar, and similar).
  Mood: huge, immovable, intimidating, dominant. Camera: VERY
  LOW ANGLE looking steeply UP at the creature with minor
  foreshortening, framing it as towering and massive to emphasize
  its size and stature. Pose energy: rearing up to full height,
  roaring, slamming down, looming over the viewer.
- WERERAT — for small, quick SKIRMISHERS (rat, bat, and similar).
  Mood: small, twitchy, frantic, sly. Camera: VERY HIGH ANGLE
  looking steeply DOWN at the creature, framing it as small and
  conveying constant darting, scurrying movement. Pose energy:
  scampering, darting low to the ground, crouched and quick,
  caught mid-run.
Commit to the SINGLE resolved archetype and stage the whole scene
(camera, mood, pose) to its bundle.

— TRANSFORMATION —

IF FULL HYBRID MODE (single image): transform the character
ENTIRELY into the hybrid werebeast of the resolved beast:
- HEAD → a FULL BEAST HEAD (muzzle/snout, beast ears, fangs or
  sharp teeth, beast nose) of the resolved animal. The human face
  is gone, BUT the eyes keep the CHARACTER'S EYE COLOR and the
  fur/mane keeps the CHARACTER'S HAIR COLOR — this is how the
  beast still reads as THEM.
- BODY → a powerful BIPEDAL hybrid frame: digitigrade or
  beast-jointed legs, clawed hands/paws, a fur-covered muscular
  body, a tail appropriate to the beast, beast-appropriate size
  (towering for Werebear, lean and tall for Werewolf, small and
  hunched for Wererat). The frame's sex/build (feminine leaner and
  graceful, masculine broader and heavier) follows the
  PRESENTATION block below.
- FUR → colored from the CHARACTER'S HAIR COLOR and overall
  palette (even if unnatural — blue hair yields blue-toned fur),
  with palette accents.
- The result is unmistakably a were-beast monstrosity, never a
  plain animal and never a human in a costume.

IF MID-TRANSFORMATION MODE (two images): render the character
from @Image1 CAUGHT MID-SHIFT toward the beast from @Image2 — a
single figure in the violent in-between:
- the face partway shifted (jaw elongating into a forming muzzle,
  beast eyes emerging in the character's eye color, fangs
  growing, fur spreading across the skin)
- the body contorting and bulking, claws erupting from the hands,
  fur erupting across exposed skin, the spine and limbs reshaping
- still clearly recognizable as the @Image1 character mid-change,
  blended with @Image2's beast features
- staged for drama and the archetype's mood, the transformation
  itself the focus of the moment.

ABSOLUTE HEAD NOTE: in FULL HYBRID mode the beast head SUPERSEDES
any mask, helm, hood, or face-covering the source wore (it is torn
away or absorbed by the change) and forms even for headless/
smoke-headed sources. If the source has no visible eye color to
carry, choose a fitting eye color from the character's palette.

— IDENTITY (carried through theme, not the human form) —
Because the character becomes a beast, identity is carried by:
the EYE COLOR (kept on the beast's eyes), the HAIR COLOR (becomes
the fur/mane color), the overall COLOR PALETTE, and the TORN
TRANSFORMED CLOTHING (below). Signature accessories (a pendant, a
band, a symbol) may survive on the beast, hanging or partly
broken. The viewer should sense WHO this was, now a werebeast.

— PRESENTATION (SEX/BUILD-GATED) —
Determine the character's sex/build:
- If {PRESENTATION} is provided, use it exactly.
- If blank, READ IT FROM @Image1'S BODY AND PHYSICAL BUILD. Note:
  image models DEFAULT werebeasts to a masculine, hulking build,
  so a feminine read must be applied DELIBERATELY — it will NOT
  happen on its own.
Then shape the werebeast's anatomy accordingly, layered OVER the
archetype's size:
- FEMININE: give the werebeast a clearly FEMININE build and
  silhouette — a leaner, more slender and graceful frame, narrower
  shoulders, a tapered waist, finer and more agile limbs, a softer
  and finer muzzle and facial structure, and a fuller, flowing
  mane or ruff echoing the character's hairstyle. She is
  unmistakably female AND savage: fierce, wild, and powerful, a
  lethal predatory huntress-beast (a sleek lioness, not a bulky
  male lion). For a Werebear she is still large but with a clearly
  female frame; for a Werewolf or Wererat she is lithe and
  graceful. The feminine read comes from STRUCTURE, SILHOUETTE,
  PROPORTIONS, facial fineness, and mane — never from sexualized
  anatomy or exposed skin.
- MASCULINE: a heavier, broader, more muscular and rugged frame, a
  stronger blockier muzzle, a coarser mane.
- NEUTRAL/AMBIGUOUS: an androgynous beast build between the two.
This clause is about anatomical STRUCTURE and silhouette only.
Keep it fully SFW — no sexualized features, no suggestive posing,
no skin-reveal; the beast is fur-covered. Feminine and SFW are NOT
in conflict: a feminine werebeast is conveyed through build,
proportions, fineness, and grace, not through sexualization.

— CLOTHING (TRANSFORMED, THEN TORN) —
First TRANSFORM the character's clothing into authentic
high-fantasy medieval Dungeons & Dragons adventurer attire (real
fantasy garments/armor as worn in a medieval fantasy world) —
modern, contemporary, casual, sci-fi, or non-medieval clothing
MUST be redesigned as its medieval-fantasy counterpart (a hoodie
becomes a leather jerkin or cloak; jeans and a t-shirt become a
tunic and trousers; a suit becomes a doublet) — keeping the
original's COLOR SCHEME, thematic character, patterns, motifs,
and signature pieces. THEN show that fantasy attire being violently
TORN, ripped, and shredded by the transformation: straining,
splitting at the seams, hanging in tatters off the changing body,
in the original colors. The clothing is fantasy adventurer gear
mid-destruction, never intact modern clothing.

— POSE (ARCHETYPE-DRIVEN — ABSOLUTE OVERRIDE OF SOURCE STANCE) —
Do NOT copy @Image1's (or @Image2's) pose; the source stances are
DISCARDED. Stage a NEW dynamic pose matching the resolved
archetype's energy. Choose ONE at random from the archetype's set:
- WEREWOLF: (1) mid-leap lunging at the viewer, claws leading;
  (2) slashing across the body, head snarling along the swing;
  (3) crouched low and coiled, about to spring, snarling.
- WEREBEAR: (1) reared to full towering height, arms wide,
  roaring; (2) slamming both fists down, looming forward;
  (3) standing massive and immovable, glaring down, fists
  clenched.
- WERERAT: (1) scampering low across the ground, mid-run;
  (2) darting to the side in a quick crouch, glancing back;
  (3) perched low and twitchy, about to bolt.
Preserved/transformed features (beast head, fur, claws, tail,
torn clothing, surviving accessories) re-pose dynamically WITH the

— CAMERA / DYNAMISM (SET BY ARCHETYPE) —
Use the resolved archetype's camera treatment (NOT a random pick):
- WEREWOLF → heavy foreshortening + slight motion blur on the

- WEREBEAR → very low angle looking up, minor foreshortening,
  towering/massive framing.
- WERERAT → very high angle looking down, frenetic small-and-
  scurrying framing.

— COMPOSITION —
Solo composition — ONE werebeast (or one mid-shift figure) only,
the single focal subject, framed to look powerful, feral, and
striking. The beast head, fur, claws, tail, torn clothing, and
any surviving accessories are ALL PART OF the single creature, not
separate figures. 16:9 landscape aspect ratio, composed as splash
art / key-visual, staged to the archetype's camera. Strong
silhouette clarity and cinematic depth.
